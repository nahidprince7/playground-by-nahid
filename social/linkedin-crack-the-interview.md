## 🇬🇧 English (companion)

Saying "I don't know" well is an interview skill. Nobody practises it, because there's nowhere to practise it. 😅

So I built an interview simulator. Not a quiz — a simulation.

The difference matters. A quiz asks *do you know the answer?* An interview asks whether you can hold yourself together while someone judges you. The questions are only half the game.

🎮 How it runs:

📋 Pick a role — 9 of them, junior through senior, each one built from a real job posting. React, Next.js, Java, Spring, Swift, Kotlin, Rust, AWS, Kubernetes, QA.

⏱️ You get 30 seconds with the briefing. You may revise **3 of the 6 topics**. The other three? Hope they don't come up. 🙏

🎤 Then four rounds, each with its own interviewer:
HR Screen → Technical Screen → Deep Dive → The Ask

📉 There's a meter at the top: **Interviewer Confidence**. It starts at 60%.
Good answers raise it. Wrong answers drain it. Get one wrong on a topic you *didn't* revise and it costs double.

Hit 0% and the interview ends right there. The remaining questions never get asked — exactly like real life. 💀

🆘 And three lifelines, modelling what good candidates actually do:

🔁 "Could you repeat that?" — buys 20 seconds
💭 "Let me think out loud" — removes one weak option
🤝 "I don't know — but here's how I'd find out" — skips safely, for less than any wrong answer costs

That third one is the whole point. **Being honest is always cheaper than bluffing** — in the interview, and in the job.

🛠️ Stack not on the list? Build your own interview — pick a level, pick any 5 skills, and you're in.

📊 At the end you get a debrief: your confidence graphed across all four rounds, your weakest topic with the exact questions you missed, and whether your lifelines actually paid off.

## 🎯 Posting tips (for max reach)

- **Best time:** Tue–Thu, 9–11 AM in your audience's timezone.
- **Attach media.** An 8–12 second screen recording is worth more than any paragraph here: show the confidence ring dropping after a wrong answer, or the verdict screen landing on "Rejected on the spot". Video/GIF > image > text-only for reach.
- **Don't name the companies.** The roles are built from real postings, but naming the employers in the post reads as affiliation or endorsement you don't have. "Built from real job postings" says the same thing safely — that's why the job titles in the game don't carry employer names either.
- **Post one language per update.** Or run Bangla and English a few days apart and see which lands better — this audience is split, so it's worth measuring.
- **Reply to every comment in the first hour.** Comments move distribution far more than likes, and "what verdict did you get?" is built to farm them — people love reporting a bad score.
- **Ask for the stack you're missing.** A genuine "which role should I add next?" in the comments gives you both engagement and a content roadmap.
- If reach looks throttled, move the link into the **first comment** instead of the body.
